## 0. Why prefill matters to the owner (and how much)

- Served prefill: **5.77 -> 5.62 ms/token (~178 tok/s) at 1 024-row chunks, in place (no staging), `FRANKEN_GLM_MOE_G=8`** (record §L5-GLM-G8: 5.769 -> 5.621 at 262 144 cells; handoff §1). Decode is ~60 ms a token; one prefill token costs a tenth of that, so prefill speed is invisible in a chat that hits the prefix cache.
- It shows in three places: (1) **a cold new chat** after a restart or a changed system prompt: 28.4 s for the 4 573-token tool prompt (`accept_live`, record §L5-GLM-STAGECROSS acceptance paragraph); the warm case is 0.84-0.86 s because 4 548 of 4 576 tokens are reused; (2) **a long document**: 5.6 ms x 25 000 tokens is ~140 s (computed, depth effects not included: whether ms/token grows with depth at the shipped config is not known, PF0); (3) the first message of a follow-up that pastes new material.
- So the prize is "minutes to seconds on big cold prompts", not tokens-per-second in chat. No target number has been set by the owner; items carry stop rules (section 5).

## 1. What is known about where prefill time goes (and how old it is)

- **Attribution, taken at the OLD staged config** (record §M7-SKIPCLASS: 8 192 prose tokens, chunk 512, staged experts, `--gemm-lds 1`, A,B,B,A, skip masks): everything 9.02 ms/token; trunk GEMMs worth **2.45** (from the experts-skipped state), experts 2.33, attention + indexer 0.49, KDA 0.11; the skeleton (staging DMA, norms, hyper-connection, router, boundary copies, no compute) 3.74. Classes are sub-additive (solo costs sum to 6.2 against 5.29 together). **That config is no longer served** (staging is off since 2026-10-06, chunk 1 024, blocked expert kernel, G=8), so these shares are a hypothesis for the shipped build, not a measurement of it.
- **Links:** with no compute beside them the staged DMA reached 47.6 GB/s aggregate (91 % of the 52 GB/s M4 probe); compute running beside the copies leaves the links idle (busy share 98 % -> 70-84 %) **and** slows each copy in flight (-21..-27 % per link) (§M7-SKIPCLASS). Memory/fabric clocks are not the cause (§M7-CLOCKTEST).
- **Bytes per token fall as 1/chunk:** fit P(chunk) = 7.26 + 1198/chunk ms/token (staged, §L5-GLM-CHUNK); chunk 1 024 vs 512 measured -12 %. Chunk 2 048 would be worth ~0.6 ms/token by that fit but needs ~1.2 GB/card of helper scratch restructured and the chunk-plan kernel's LDS array moved to global memory (handoff §4 item 5); the cards are ~99 % full at steady state (free MiB 732 / 818 / 458, CLAUDE.md "Before a build that allocates device memory").
- **Per-card utilisation.** At the OLD staged config the device timeline (record §L5-GLM-TIMELINE, chunk 512, 8 192 tokens) found card 0 busy 2.7 of 9.0 ms a token and the cause: the 8.4 MB embedding upload waited ~3.0 s of every ~4.4 s chunk behind the staging ring's DMAs, which gated the whole pipeline (fixed since: embedding by kernel, §L5-GLM-EMBED, prefill only -3.3 %; staging is off). So the utilisation of the SHIPPED build is not known (handoff §4 item 6 says ~50 %, "not investigated"). The engine already issues chunks without waiting (`--prefill-pipeline`, served default 1) and hands the residual between cards through **two banks** (`Scratch::hout[0|1]`, `boundary_recv(..., bank)`): the pipeline is at most two chunks deep by construction. With three cards and layers split 15 / 15 / 15 a steady-state pipeline needs three chunks in flight to keep every card busy. This is the reading to test, not a result.
- **Trunk GEMMs:** `k_gemm_lds` 1.62 + `k_gemm_batch<8,1>` 0.84 ms/token (old config); **every K-quant matrix is refused by the LDS path** and runs the generic kernel (handoff §4 item 3).
- **Placement in prefill:** held during prefill (`FRANKEN_ADAPT_PREFILL=0`, a held span counts as at most 64 tokens in the average); placement raised prefill 5.77 -> 5.62.
- **Correctness note (D14, 2026-10-08):** until tonight three rows in four of every chunk past 2 048 tokens lost their 1-3 newest keys in the DSA layers. Timing is unchanged by the fix (31.18 s for 2 300 tokens at chunk 512, old and new); any prefill claim from now on is checked against a chunk-1 reference at depth 2 300 (section 2).

## 2. How every item is measured

1. **Speed instrument:** A,B,B,A palindromes in one process, 8 192 prose tokens, 262 144 cells, shipped flags (chunk 1 024, in place, `--gemm-lds 1`, G=8, `--prefill-pipeline 1`), `--time-prefill`; the prefill device timeline (`--timeline`, chunks 3-7, analysed by the stdlib script in franken-engine's `franken/decode/`; GLM5.md section 14) for busy shares per card and link; the skip-class build (`ckpt1006/build_dbgskip.sh`, masks 1 experts, 2 trunk GEMMs, 4 KDA, 8 MLA attention, 16 indexer) for attribution; `gpu_sampler.py` beside every chain. One odd arm is not a result; name any arm left out.
2. **Exactness where nothing reorders** (scheduling, pipelining, buffers): the model gate (`glm_q8f_chain.sh` configuration set, maxabs 0) **plus the D14 gate**: a chunked prefill of a 2 300-token prompt against a chunk-1 prefill (`ckpt1006/glm_d14_chain.sh`, 1 788 of 1 788 taps). Never two chunked runs with the same last chunk: they share any fault and agree (this hid D14).
3. **Quality gate where a sum is reordered** (GEMM tile kernel, WMMA, int8 activations): `franken_quality_chain.sh` on the served engine against the previous binary (MMLU-Pro 70 + needle, same reasoning budget) AND a layer-wise error report against the bit-exact build (cos and maxabs per tap on the model-gate prompts, so a regression has a place, not only a score). The change is labelled "quality-gated, not bit-exact" from the first commit and in the record.
4. **VRAM first:** any item that allocates device memory starts from the shipped config's log (steady free MiB per card) and writes the budget before the build; optional buffers degrade, they do not abort.
5. Landing and acceptance as in the decode plan: franken-engine via the Mac clone, `glm_accept_chain.sh` + `run_ui2.sh`, the task-closeout skill; GitHub only on the owner's go-ahead after the secret scan. Tiers: Sonnet for harness and ports of an existing pattern, Opus for a real kernel; subagents never start a GPU program.

## 4. Order and expected total

1. **PF0 first, always** (1 day). It is cheap and it either confirms the 50 %-idle / trunk-GEMM story or kills it.
2. Then, by PF0's ranking: PF1 if the cards idle and the links do not; PF2 if the trunk GEMMs are still >= 1.5 ms/token; PF3 if the links idle or slow under compute; PF5 as a measurement beside either.
3. PF4 and PF6 only if their targets appear in PF0. PF7/PF8 parked.
- **Realistic total, if PF0 confirms the old shares:** PF1 and PF2 together could take 1-2 ms off 5.6 (20-35 %) -- an **estimate** with the per-card idle share and the GEMM speed-up as its two unknowns; if PF0 says the links are the limit, the answer is "little", and the plan ends at PF3/PF5 with a record entry. Outside anecdotes put 300-400 tok/s as realistic with host-resident experts against ~178 now (section 6; not measured on this box).

## 5. Stop rules

- PF1 stops if PF0 shows the cards busy >= 85 % or the links the binding resource (a pipeline deeper than the links can feed gains nothing).
- PF2 stops if the microbenchmark does not reach 1.5x `k_gemm_lds`'s TFLOPs on the K-quant shapes, or the quality gate shows a drop outside the Wilson interval of the previous binary on MMLU-Pro or any needle miss that the old binary got right.
- PF3, PF4: stop if the predicted gain is < 0.3 ms/token after PF0.
- Any item whose exactness gate shows a non-exact tap in a change that claims "no bit moves" is fixed or dropped, never shipped behind a quality claim; a deliberate order change goes through section 2 rule 3 from the start.
- A speed claim needs the timeline or A,B,B,A palindrome AND the serve-path number (`accept_live` cold new chat, a 25 k-token document through the gateway). One odd arm is not a result.

## 6. Outside prior art (a research pass on 2026-10-08 night: Reddit RSS, GitHub issues / PRs / source read directly; 'author-reported' and '(unverified)' are the pass's own labels; nothing here was run on `rome`)

**Several chunks in flight across layer-split cards (PF1).** llama.cpp's pipeline parallelism keeps up to `LLAMA_SCHED_MAX_COPIES` (4) copies of the scheduler inputs so micro-batches overlap across GPUs: 8x A100-PCIe, 7B F16 ~2.4x on 4 GPUs; a quantised model gained less (Q4_0 at pp4096 ~1.3x / 2.1x / 3.2x on 2 / 4 / 8 GPUs; ratios are the pass's arithmetic from the PR table) -- ggml-org/llama.cpp PR 6017. In current master it is switched off whenever tensor overrides exist (`--cpu-moe`, `--n-cpu-moe`); whether it is active on HIP is unverified. **TurboPrefill** (github.com/sergey-automation/TurboPrefill, CUDA only, author-reported): replays consecutive ubatches as overlapping waves, 1.6-3x over stock layer-split pipelining at 16 K context (4x3090 Llama-70B 400 -> 1 208 tok/s, GPT-OSS-120B 1 477 -> 2 758), best with a SMALLER ubatch (128 against 1 024), no gain below ~4 K tokens; no ROCm version is public. **SGLang chunked pipeline parallelism** (lmsys.org blog 2026-01-15): the next chunk starts once the previous chunk's hidden states are sent, async P2P on separate compute and copy streams, > 80 % scaling efficiency to PP4 (H20), bubble ratio (P - 1) / (P - 1 + M) with M chunks. Nothing found for TensorRT-LLM; vLLM lists it as a later optimisation (issue 11945), shipping unconfirmed.

**What that means here (arithmetic, not a result).** With P = 3 cards the bubble is 2 / (2 + M): a 2 300-token prompt at 1 024-row chunks is M = 3 -> 40 %; the 4.6 k-token tool prompt M = 5 -> 29 %; a 25 k-token document M = 25 -> 7 %. So deeper pipelining pays on big cold documents and little on the tool prompt; and TurboPrefill's preference for small ubatches pulls against the 1/chunk expert bytes (§L5-GLM-CHUNK), which pipelining may change because the three cards' links then work at the same time instead of one after another.

**Prefill GEMM on gfx1100 (PF2).** llama.cpp uses int8-WMMA MMQ on RDNA3 for most types and switches Q6_K and Q2_K to dequantise-then-hipBLAS above batch 128 (`ggml_cuda_should_use_mmq`, "MMQ can have lower peak TOPS"). PR 17576 on a W7900 (gfx1100), ub512, pp2048: WMMA-MMQ beat the dp4a path on Q4_0 (1 980 -> 2 221 tok/s), Q8_0 (1 891 -> 2 228), Q4_K_S (1 958 -> 2 109); Q6_K fell to 0.75x and Q2_K to 0.67x. PR 17495: int8 WMMA on RDNA3 runs at the same ops per cycle as fp16, so the gain is registers and bandwidth. **stew675/llama-cpp-rdna-boosts** (`archive/work/mmb-general/gfx1100-s5s7-results.md`): 'mmb', a tile GEMM that dequantises weights to bf16 and feeds WMMA: on a 7900 XTX at pp8192 +14.4 % over int8 MMQ on a 27B UD-Q4_K_M (1 022 -> 1 170 tok/s, ~55 -> 63 TFLOP/s), +11.3 % on a 12B Q8_0; a double-buffered big tile does not fit the 64 KB LDS; output text changes, perplexity on par; the set also has a fused MoE gate + up + GLU MMQ for Q3_K-Q6_K and Q8_0 and a bf16 chunked gated-delta-net kernel (+4 % prefill on gfx1100). Nothing found on measured hipBLASLt or WMMA TFLOPs on a 7900 XTX (spec peak 123 TFLOPS). Our generic K-quant kernel is slower than MMQ, so the gain on the 2.45 ms trunk could exceed the +11-14 % (unverified).

**MoE prefill with host-resident experts (PF3, PF4, PF5).** llama.cpp issue 25859 (3060, PCIe 4): the GPU sat idle 42 % of prefill; page-locking the mmap'd experts +21 %; a second upload stream with a few staging slots 1 143 -> 1 880 tok/s; at pp2048 the batch selects essentially all 256 experts, so reading routing ids back was pointless (our in-place path has no such read). PR 21067 (weight prefetch, unmerged): +14.7 % on 2x3090 but 150 -> 95 tok/s on a PCIe 3 card at ub2048. **KTransformers** layerwise GPU prefill streams each whole layer's experts through two CPU staging slots above a token threshold (it has a GLM-5.3-Flash tutorial). The OSDI'26 stream-loading prefill (arXiv 2606.10493) runs layer by layer over the WHOLE prompt with a loader, a model thread and an unloader: DeepSeek-V3 FP8 1 200 tok/s on one RTX 5090 (PCIe 5), 1 800 on two, no gain at 4 K prompts. llama.cpp's merged expert-cache PR 29887 is decode-only. Fiddler, MoE-Lightning, MoE-Infinity, Klotski: only search summaries read, nothing prefill-specific for PCIe 4 (unverified).

**Zero-copy reads against copies (PF3).** EMOGI (arXiv 2006.06890, V100, PCIe 3): warp-coalesced, 128-byte-aligned zero-copy reads reach 12.23 GB/s, the same as `cudaMemcpy`; misaligned warps split into extra requests and lose; ~135 requests in flight suffice. Nothing found that quantifies compute slowing a concurrent DMA, or SDMA against blit kernels on gfx1100. Knobs only: `HSA_ENABLE_SDMA` (H2D, D2H, D2D), `HSA_ENABLE_PEER_SDMA` (D2D, ignored when SDMA is off); CLR defaults `GPU_MAX_HW_QUEUES=4`, `DEBUG_CLR_LIMIT_BLIT_WG=16`; second-hand MI250X slides say SDMA caps near 49 GB/s but overlaps compute, blit is faster but does not overlap (unverified for gfx1100). (We already tried 16 hardware queues: §L5-GLM-TIMELINE H1b, no gain.)

**Sparse and linear attention at long prompts (PF6).** The model has 34 KDA and 11 KPool-DSA layers, the indexer pools 4 tokens; llama.cpp's port (PR 27773) adds no new ops and uses sparse flash-attention for DSA prefill. vLLM fuses the DeepSeek-V3.2 indexer top-k because the full logits tensor was materialised first; SGLang reuses indexer results across layers (IndexCache, via search summary, unverified). FlashKDA is 1.72-2.22x the FLA KDA kernel on H20 (SM90 only); a llama.cpp CUDA chunked gated-delta-net prefill kernel gave +5-19 % for the whole model (PR 26001, closed unmerged). KDA is ~0.1 ms/token here, so low priority; nothing found for AMD.

**Realistic targets (anecdotal, GLM-5.3-Flash).** Strix Halo with all weights in unified memory 580 tok/s at 3.5 K and 546 at 64 K (r/LocalLLaMA 1wwocik); M3 Ultra Q4 ~550, 4x3090 'no offload' ~600 (comment, anecdotal); IQ3_S on an RTX 4090 + 128 GB DDR5 ~300 tok/s at 256 K with `-ub 2048` (llama.cpp PR 27773). The pass's judgment: **300-400 tok/s is realistic with host-resident experts against our ~178; above 500 needs the experts in VRAM.**

**Ranked by the pass, for our case:** (1) several chunks in flight across the three cards; (2) a bf16-WMMA dequantising tile GEMM for the K-quants; (3) layer-major prefill with a larger MoE batch than the attention chunk (stream-loading / KTransformers; a design inference of the pass, VRAM-limited here, see PF4); (4) hygiene: 128-byte-aligned warp reads, one merged `hipHostRegister` range per tensor, queue flags (small; the +21 % of the pinning came from a staged-copy path).
