## 3. How we got here, and why

**F0 (09-17, PASS).** Question: must streaming experts to the cards move to
HIP? No. On the backend's own queue family Vulkan copies at 60–63 GB/s across
three cards, HIP's number. Two facts it left for F2: amdgpu refuses to import
page-cache memory, so experts go through a staging buffer; and a
single-thread fill of that buffer (15 GB/s), not PCIe, is the bottleneck.

**F4 (09-17, ANSWERED).** Why does a token cost 205 ms more at 18k depth?
Per-request `[OPTIME]` in engine mode (now a standing tool, `COLI_TIMERS=1`)
showed the DSA indexer was 275 ms of a 509 ms forward. That opened F6.

**F6 design → F6a (09-18/19, in service).** The Opus design found the
indexer was not a numerics problem: a single-threaded scalar score loop and
a quadratic top-512. F6a = OpenMP over the pools plus an exact heap top-k,
bit-identical, done by Sonnet. Decode at 18k 2.27 → 4.33, the 18k turn
2 607 → 1 179 s. The design also corrected the plan: F2's "113.9 ms of
experts" had been the whole prefill token, so F2's 10× promise was
withdrawn and its gate re-based to 1.6× / 2.0×. **Incident:** a 96 GiB
`external_memory_host` probe killed at its budget left a thread in
uninterruptible sleep in amdgpu with 96 GiB pinned; it needed a reboot. Do
not import large anonymous regions into Vulkan on this box (it is also
slow: 35 → 10.7 GB/s from 8 to 64 GiB).

**F2 (09-19, in service).** Prefill streams non-resident experts into a
host-RAM ring (a ring in ReBAR VRAM filled at 16 GB/s against 58.6) and runs
them on the GPU tile kernels; chunk 512. The agent reported PASS without
running the KL bar. Fable ran `kl_compare` on the dumps it had left: top-1
95.05 %, FAIL. Cause: not F2 at all — the routed-expert GPU kernel had
never applied GLM's swiglu clamp, and streaming moved more experts onto it.
Fix: `GLM53_VK_SWIGLU_CLAMP=1`, a second pair of shader modules; under the
clamp stream on/off is KL 3.5e-05, 99.89 %. **Lesson: a gate that was not
run is not a pass; read the chain's output, not the agent's summary.**
Prefix checkpoints from before that day carry unclamped numerics and were
set aside (`<model>/.coli_ckpt.pre-f2.*`, 1.3 GB, deletable).

**F1 (09-19, REJECTED).** Stream CPU misses in decode too? Step 0 split the
overlapping eg/cpu timers: CPU misses are the critical path, ceiling +29 %.
The probe then measured a streamed batch-1 window at 2.70 ms against 1.747
on the CPU. Dead on its own probe, before any build. That also kept F5 dead
(see below).

**F7 (09-19/20, in service).** The prefill attention core on dev0, four
shaders, Opus. 18k turn 683 → 455 s (1.50×). Its numerics took a night: it
moved ~2 % of argmaxes against the CPU path. The agent's explanation ("the
CPU is the less accurate side") was refuted by a float64 arm Fable added
(`GLM53_MLA_ATTN_REF64`). Fable's counter-suspicion (a kernel defect) was
refuted by a second arm, `GLM53_MLA_ATTN_JITTER=6e-7`: perturb the CPU path
by fp32-rounding-sized noise and it moves the same 2 %, same KL, same first
position (1420). The model is that sensitive at near-ties; the kernel is
fine. **The jitter arm is the yardstick for any reordered kernel; a float64
reference keeps the old summation order and flatters the old path.**

**F5 (MTP, DEFERRED, never run).** Its precondition was a cheaper verify
block. F1's probe showed a small row block is slower streamed than on the
CPU. F8 later made a CPU miss 20 % cheaper; nobody has re-priced the verify
block with that. It is arithmetic from §F8 and §F1-PROBE, no rig.

**F8 (09-20, in service).** The batch-1 CPU expert, the largest decode
bucket. Step 0 took three rounds on a microbenchmark and refuted two
hypotheses (bandwidth-bound; one FMA latency chain — Fable's, worth 10 %)
before finding the real one: with group size 64 every group pays a scalar
`hsum` + `fmaf`. `coli_i4_row_gv` keeps group sums in vector form; one
OpenMP team per MoE layer instead of three. Decode +6.4 to +9.6 % at every
depth. A unit test caught a real bug on the way (a thread-local scale table
that was NULL in worker threads). Gap: the chain's KL report is vacuous for
a decode-only change, because streamed prefill runs no CPU expert.

**F9 → F9a (09-20, in service).** F9 was to be the indexer on the GPU, Opus.
Before commissioning it Fable read the score loop: a scalar float dot GCC
cannot vectorise — the disease P5.1 had cured in attention. F9a puts the
heads in the SIMD lanes, each dot keeps its order, bit-identical down to
33 495 index rows and a 5.6 GB logit dump. 18k turn 455 → 358 s, decode
+8.3 %. Sonnet did it; the GPU indexer was closed unbuilt. **Read the hot
loop yourself before paying for a shader.**

**F10 step 0 (09-20, fix parked).** Fable's brief said KDA prefill ran on
the CPU; wrong, and the agent corrected it: projections and recurrence are
both on the GPU, and the recurrence is S sequential dispatches behind
barriers (0.111 ms per row-layer, 33.9 s of the turn). See §2.

## 5. The working method that held up

- One item = one `perf/...` branch = one chain (`f*_gate_chain.sh`, copy the
  newest) launched through `run_chain.sh`: lock, stop gateway, oracle by
  `cmp`, ladder A,B,B,A on one binary, `gate_ab_verdict`, restart on every
  exit path, `accept_live`. A full 18k ladder is ~75 minutes.
- Serve with a wrapper like `f8_serve.sh` / `f9a_serve.sh`, then run
  `accept_ui.sh` from the Mac before telling the owner anything works.
- Land: merge to `hot-expert-tier`, record §, plan rev (look up the top rev
  first), plan §8.3 row, prefill roadmap rev + title line, `CLAUDE.md` if a
  knob or binary changed, `doc_currency.sh`, push `origin`, sync the rig
  through `p0-sync`, update memory.
- Fable launches and polls every rig job, detached
  (`ssh -n -f … setsid nohup … &`). Agents build and write; they never wait
  on the rig. Step 0 (a microbenchmark or a reading) before every build: it
  killed F1, shrank F9 and corrected F10 for almost nothing.
- Tools added on this track: `kl_compare.py`, `flip_margin.py` (are the
  moved argmaxes near-ties?), `f7_ctxdiff.py`, `f7_klprofile.py`, the
  microbenchmarks `f2_ring_probe.c`, `f7_attn_probe.c`,
  `f8_cpu_expert_bench.c`, `f9a_index_bench.c`, `f10_kda_bench.c`.
