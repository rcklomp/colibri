## Card 1: per-expert bytes, int4-gs64 vs row-wise int8, and the three-card split

**Measured**, by reading the safetensors headers of expert 0, layer 0, in
both containers (`~/models/qwen36_i4_gs64`, complete; `~/models/qwen36_i8_row`,
partial but its layer-0 shard was already written):

| | int4-gs64 (`qwen36_i4_gs64`) | row-wise int8 (`qwen36_i8_row`, `--ebits 8`, no `--gs`) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `merged_weight` dtype/shape | `U8` `[1 572 864]` (packed, 2 nibbles/byte) | `I8` `[3 145 728]` |
| `merged_weight` bytes | 1 572 864 | 3 145 728 |
| `qs` dtype/shape | `F32` `[49 152]` (32 or 8 groups/row, gs=64) | `F32` `[3 072]` (1 scale/row) |
| `qs` bytes | 196 608 | 12 288 |
| **bytes/expert** | **1 769 472 (1.6875 MiB)** | **3 158 016 (3.0117 MiB)** |

Ratio int8/int4 = **1.785×** — not 2×, because `qs` shrinks 16× (one scale
per row instead of one per 64-wide group) while `merged_weight` doubles
exactly. `qwen36_meta.json`'s `expert_gs` field (`c/qwen36.c:602`, read at
`:1189`) is `64` for the first container and `0` for the second (confirmed
in the converter: `c/tools/convert_qwen36.py:540`,
`meta["expert_gs"] = args.gs`, and `--gs` defaults to 0 — "row-wise int8" is
`expert_gs=0`, matching CLAUDE.md's "`expert_gs` absent = per-row").

Total experts, both containers: 40 layers × 256 experts = **10 240** (`qwen36_meta.json`:
`n_layers=40`, `num_experts=256`, both containers — the router/topk/layer
count are unquantized architecture, identical either way).

| | total expert bytes | as GB (decimal) | as GiB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| int4-gs64 | 18 119 393 280 | **18.12 GB** | 16.88 GiB |
| row-wise int8 | 32 338 083 840 | **32.34 GB** | 30.12 GiB |

The int4 figure matches V1's own measured log line verbatim
(`[qtier-vk] planned 10240 experts (18.12 GB of a 24.72 GB budget)`,
record §V1) — this table is the same arithmetic one layer down.

**Card capacity (measured):** all three cards report identical VRAM,
`/sys/class/drm/card{0,1,2}/device/mem_info_vram_total` = 25 753 026 560 bytes
each = **25.75 GB decimal = 23.98 GiB** (i.e. a 24 GiB card, GB-vs-GiB
labelling applies here too: the box's RAM habit of quoting GiB as "GB" does
not apply to VRAM, which sysfs reports in true bytes). With the 1.0 GB
reserve V1 already uses (glm53's G6 rule, `COLI_VK_TIER_RESERVE_GB`,
`c/qwen36_tier_vk.c:199-201`) and the switch-model precondition V1's own
chain asserts (VRAM < 1 GB used per card before the run starts — i.e. no
other engine resident), each card's usable budget is **24.75 GB** (V1's own
log rounds this to 24.72 for dev3 specifically, with a small nonzero
baseline `used_gb` at that moment; both are the same arithmetic to two
significant figures).

| | int4-gs64 (18.12 GB) | row-wise int8 (32.34 GB) |
|---|---|---|
| fits on ONE card? | **Yes**, with 6.63 GB to spare (24.75 − 18.12) | No — 1.31× one card's budget |
| fits on TWO cards? | trivially | **Yes**, with 17.17 GB to spare (2×24.75 − 32.34) |
| fits on THREE cards? | trivially, 55.63 GB to spare | trivially, 41.92 GB to spare |

So **int4 needs one card and int8 needs two** (of three), for the expert set
alone — this is the header-arithmetic answer to "is 8-bit ever forced to a
new device-count", independent of the KL question the chain above will
answer.

## Card 2: non-expert weights, KV bytes/token, DeltaNet state/slot, and how much of dev0 that leaves

**Measured**, summing every non-expert tensor's `data_offsets` across all 41
shards of `qwen36_i4_gs64` (script: read each shard's safetensors header,
skip any key containing `.experts.`, sum the rest):

| component | bytes | GB |
|---|---:|---:|
| `embed_tokens` + `lm_head` (F16) | 2 034 237 440 | 2.034 |
| DeltaNet (`linear_attn.*`, all 30 linear-attention layers, F16) | 2 023 107 840 | 2.023 |
| shared expert (`mlp.shared_expert*`, F16) | 251 822 080 | 0.252 |
| router (`mlp.gate.weight`, F16) | 41 943 040 | 0.042 |
| full attention (`self_attn.{q,k,v,o}_proj`, 10 full-attention layers, F16) | 344 981 504 | 0.345 |
| norms | 342 016 | 0.0003 |
| **total non-expert** | **4 896 711 936** | **4.90 GB (4.56 GiB)** |

**Checked, not assumed, that this number is the same in both containers**:
layer 0's non-expert keys (`input_layernorm`, `linear_attn.*`, `mlp.gate`,
`mlp.shared_expert*`, `post_attention_layernorm` — 16 tensors) have identical
key sets, dtypes (`F16`) and shapes between `qwen36_i4_gs64` and the partial
`qwen36_i8_row`, and so does the whole `model-globals.safetensors` shard
(`embed_tokens`, `lm_head`, `norm` — 3 tensors, `F16`, identical shapes).
`c/tools/convert_qwen36.py` writes every non-`experts.` key with
`get_tensor(k).half()` (line ~471) regardless of `--ebits` — attention,
router, shared expert, DeltaNet, embeddings and the final norm are **F16 in
both containers, unconditionally**; only `make_merged()` (the routed-expert
path) branches on `ebits`. So step 0's "differ only in routed experts"
premise is confirmed by the converter's own control flow, not inferred from
one layer's worth of headers.

**Container total size, both (measured non-expert + measured/derived
expert)**: int4-gs64 ≈ 18.12 + 4.90 = **23.02 GB** (the directory measures
~22 GiB on disk, consistent to rounding + the `.cache`/tokenizer overhead);
row-wise int8 ≈ 32.34 + 4.90 = **37.23 GB** — close to, but not the same
quantity as, the owner's original 37.5 GB estimate for the official
`Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-FP8` repo (rev 33): that is FP8 (2 bytes-ish/element
after HF's own packing) from a different source tree; this is our own
row-wise INT8 container. The closeness is coincidental, not a cross-check.

**KV cache (measured from the engine, `c/qwen36.c`):** `ensure_kv()`
(`c/qwen36.c:2447-2467`) allocates `K`/`V` as **`float` (`falloc`, 4 bytes)**,
one array pair per **full-attention** layer only (`is_attn[i]`; DeltaNet
layers carry no KV, they carry the recurrent state instead), sized
`kv_heads * max_t * k_head_dim` each. From `qwen36_meta.json`: `kv_heads=2`,
`k_head_dim=v_head_dim=256` (both dequantized from `k_proj`/`v_proj` shapes
divided by `kv_heads`, `c/tools/convert_qwen36.py` lines ~522-527), 10
full-attention layers (`layer_types`, every 4th of 40).

    bytes/token = 2 (K,V) x kv_heads(2) x k_head_dim(256) x 4 (fp32) x 10 (full-attn layers)
                = 40 960 bytes = 40 KiB/token, EXACTLY

This is today's number for the CPU engine (KV never leaves host RAM in the
served V1 binary — `qt_place_of` returns `QT_PLACE_CPU` unconditionally,
`c/qwen36_tier_vk.c:124`). It is what "KV on dev0" would have to carry if
ported unchanged.

**DeltaNet recurrent + conv state per slot (measured from the engine,
`c/qwen36.c:1362-1373`):** `dn_vheads=32, dn_kdim=128, dn_vdim=128` gives a
`[32,128,128]` f32 recurrent state per linear-attention layer (2 097 152
bytes), plus a `dn_conv_dim(8192) x (dn_convk-1)(3)` f32 conv ring
(98 304 bytes) — **2 195 456 bytes/layer**, fixed size regardless of context
length (it is a recurrence, not a cache), over the 30 linear-attention
layers:

    DeltaNet state/slot = 30 x 2 195 456 = 65 863 680 bytes = 62.81 MiB

**Projected: what dev0 could hold.** If the non-expert weights moved to
dev0 unquantized (F16, no new int8-trunk kernel — the conservative case),
dev0's 24.75 GB budget has **19.86 GB** left for KV + DeltaNet state
(24.75 − 4.90). At `--kv-slots 4` (the number the served `glm53` gateway
uses today, not yet a qwen36 concept — there are no KV slots on this engine,
record §V1 "Multi-request identity"): DeltaNet's fixed cost is
4 × 62.81 MiB = 0.26 GB, leaving 19.59 GB for KV, i.e. **≈119 600 tokens of
KV per slot** at 4 slots, or **≈483 000 tokens** in a single-slot design.
Both exceed `config.json`'s own `max_position_embeddings: 262 144` — i.e.
**KV capacity on dev0 is not the constraint even at the model's full trained
context, in either slot count**, the same conclusion CLAUDE.md already
records for GLM's KV at the depths this box reaches in a session. This
number is **identical for the int4 and int8 expert cases**: dev0's budget
above never contains a single routed expert in either scenario (int4 fits
on one of the other two cards, int8 on two of them), so which expert format
is chosen does not change what dev0 can hold.

## Card 3: what `qwen36_tier_vk.c` assumes about "one device", and what three cards would actually buy

**Single-device assumptions, read directly, no code changed** (all in
`c/qwen36_tier_vk.c` unless noted):

- **File header, lines 8-17**: states the design choice outright — "ONE
  device... there is no device loop and no home(eid) hash: everything lands
  on the card `COLI_VK_DEV3` names."
- **No per-device state.** The global `G` struct (lines 59-77) has a single
  `budget`/`used` (not an array), a single flat `slot[nl*ne]` (one VRAM home
  per expert, not one per device), and a single `is_x`/`is_y` staging buffer
  for `qt_issue`/`qt_take`.
- **`qt_init` (lines 136-243) calls exactly one device's init/mem-budget
  pair**: `coli_vk_init_dev3()` (line 182), `coli_vk_mem_budget3()`
  (line 205) — never `coli_vk_init_dev2`/`coli_vk_mem_budget2` (both exist,
  `c/backend_vulkan.h:134-145`, and are exactly what `glm53` uses for its
  second expert-only device), and never the unsuffixed dev0 functions
  (`coli_vk_init`, `coli_vk_mem_budget`, `coli_vk_tensor_ensure`,
  `coli_vk_expert_group_issue`/`_take` — all exist too,
  `c/backend_vulkan.h:19,32,68-75`, and `glm53` uses dev0 for its own
  attention/dense work).
- **`qt_plan_fill`/`qt_note_planned`/`qt_issue`/`qt_take` (lines 285-438)
  only call the `*3`-suffixed backend entry points** (`coli_vk_tensor_ensure3`,
  `coli_vk_expert_group_issue3`, `coli_vk_expert_group_take3`) — there is no
  dispatch on which device an expert lives on, because every resident expert
  lives on the same one.
- **Only one env knob is read for device selection**, `COLI_VK_DEV3`
  (line 180); there is no `COLI_VK_DEV0`/`COLI_VK_DEV2` handling in this
  file at all (glm53 reads all three; qwen36's CUDA tier, next bullet, reads
  a variable-length list).
- **The trunk placement stubs (lines 124-134) always return CPU** — `qt_place_of`,
  `qt_dnproj_init/matmul`, `qt_lmhead_init/matmul` are no-ops in the Vulkan
  tier. "KV/attention on dev0" is not a small extension of this file; it is
  an unbuilt mechanism (the file's own comment, lines 119-123, calls the CUDA
  side's version of this "a second, independent mechanism").

**A multi-device pattern already exists in this repo and was not ported: the
CUDA tier, `c/qwen36_tier.c`.** It supports up to `QT_MAX_DEV=8` devices
(line 15), assigns an expert to one with `home(eid) = eid % G.ndev`
(line 63, a modulo hash — exactly the "home(eid) hash" the Vulkan file's
header says was dropped for being unneeded at one device), keeps **per-device**
budget/used arrays (`G.dev[]`, `G.budget[]`, `G.used[]`, e.g. lines 169, 201,
577-583) and a real trunk-placement auto-planner (`qt_place_of`,
`G_auto_lmh`, `G_auto_dnp[]`, lines 420-430) that puts `lm_head` and the
DeltaNet projections on whichever device has room. There is also an existing
test for exactly this shape on the fake backend:
`tests/test_qwen36_tier_multidev.c` (`c/Makefile:1722`, "same fake backend,
two devices: proves the tier's [multi-device logic]"). **Extending the
Vulkan tier from one device to three is therefore a port of an existing,
tested pattern (`home()` + per-device budget arrays, onto the already-present
`*2`/`*3`/unsuffixed backend triplet), not a new design** — which is why the
plan rates F3 step 1 "Opus, 1-2 weeks" for the *submit-model* work (step 2)
rather than for this porting step specifically; step 1 itself looks
Sonnet-shaped by the same "port a pattern that already exists" standard the
rest of this track uses.

**What three cards actually buy, evidence only:**

- **At int4, nothing on the capacity axis.** The whole expert set already
  fits on ONE card with 6.63 GB to spare (Card 1, above) — this is not a
  projection, V1 measured it. A second and third card are not needed to
  hold int4 experts today.
- **At int4, the measured overhead of NOT parallelising was already tiny.**
  Record §V1: one `qt_issue`/`qt_take` pair per layer, 40 submits/token,
  "about 2% of the token" at the driver's own measured ~25 µs/submit — the
  remaining 98% of a decode token is unattributed CPU cost (attention, dense,
  DeltaNet recurrence, none of it on a GPU today). Splitting the expert set
  across cards would add per-device submit/fence overhead to something that
  is not compute-bound at batch 1 with only `topk=8` experts active; nothing
  measured here says parallel expert dispatch buys speed, and the plan's own
  §8.2 makes the same point for GLM's resident regime ("TP adds a 25 µs hop
  per layer for a bandwidth split a 7900 XTX does not need at batch 1").
- **What a second/third card DOES plausibly buy, unmeasured:** (a) capacity
  headroom for int8 (Card 1: int8 needs two cards, not one — so *if* the KL
  chain says int8 is worth building, three cards is not optional, it is the
  minimum for compute-in-VRAM plus the router/KV working set); (b) a place
  to put the trunk (embeddings, attention, DeltaNet projections, lm_head)
  and KV in VRAM at all, which today runs entirely on the CPU and is
  unmeasured on this engine — dev0 is completely idle in the served V1
  binary; (c) the P7/P9-style multi-slot serving the plan's F3 row names,
  which needs somewhere to put per-slot KV/DeltaNet state if it is to live
  off the CPU. None of (a)-(c) is a speed number yet; they are capacity and
  architecture answers, not throughput ones.

## Result (added by Fable after the chain ran, 2026-09-20 14:11-14:21 UTC) -- int4-gs64 costs quality against row-wise int8; build the int8 tier

Chain run `f3s0kl09201411` (tree `da50a15`, binary `qwen36.f3s0` sha256
`168023797188f8e8`, byte-identical to V1's `qwen36.v1cpu`), 8 threads pinned,
both containers warm (majflt 0/1), gateway down 14:11-14:17 UTC,
`accept_live` PASS. A first launch (`f3s0kl09201409`) REFUSED itself: one
`cat` left the int8 container 84.9 % resident (pages read once sit on the
inactive list and are reclaimed ahead of GLM's cache); the chain now warms in
two passes. KL by `f3s0_kl.sh` outside the lock, ref = int8, cand = int4.
All **measured**:

| packet | positions | mean KL | max KL | top-1 agreement | flips within 0.02 of a tie | greedy text |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| short prompt | 22 | 0.0353 | 0.244 | 90.91 % (20/22) | 1 of 2 | diverges in sentence 2, both correct |
| ladder packet | 625 | **0.0316** | 0.581 | **92.96 %** (581/625) | 22 of 44 (39 of 44 within 0.10) | diverges |

For scale, this track's own bars: tier on/off on this engine 1e-10 / 100 %
(V1); stream on/off on GLM 3.5e-05 / 99.89 % ("same"); the missing swiglu
clamp 0.0233 / 91.95 % (a DEFECT, fixed). **int4-gs64 against int8 sits at
the defect's level.** Half the flips are near-ties on low-confidence
positions (median int8 P(top1) among flips 0.18), which is what 4-bit noise
looks like and not a bug -- but it is a cost, paid on every token, and rev
34's rule was "8-bit only if step 0's KL says int4 costs quality". It does.

Not measured: int8 against BF16 (the engine has no BF16 expert path).
Row-wise int8 over 2048/512-wide rows is taken as the reference on the usual
grounds, not on a number from this box.

The comparison is valid: the engine log reads `expert format on disk: int8
(tier fmt=1)` for arm B and `int4 packed ... unpacking to int8 in slot` for
arm A (which is why peak RSS is the same in both arms, 28-30 GB); everything
but the routed experts is F16 in both containers (Card 2).

**What this corrects in the plan.** Rev 33/34 said 8-bit "needs a new int8
tier kernel". It does not: `c/shaders/qmatmul*.comp` already carry
`fmt 1 = int8` row-wise, and the engine already labels the container `tier
fmt=1`. What int8 needs is what Card 1 and Card 3 say: two expert cards
instead of one (32.34 GB), i.e. the CUDA tier's `home(eid)` + per-device
budget pattern ported onto the `*2`/`*3` backend entry points. Sonnet-sized.
CPU speed is a wash (packet decode 1.93 tok/s int4, 2.06 int8, one sample
each, teacher-forcing dumps on -- not a benchmark).

  (`c/qwen36.c:1493-1502`, already proved robust against a mislabeled
